Build it yourself · Working draft

Build your own balcony irrigation controller

Start with one zone, prove every connection, and expand to five. The ESP32 owns the watering logic; solar power, a display, telemetry, and this dashboard can be added independently.

Danger—read before doing any work

Building electronic projects is hazardous and requires appropriate knowledge, tools, supervision, and continuous verification. Solar equipment and batteries can supply very high fault currents. Correct component ratings, polarity, fusing, wire sizing, insulation, ventilation, mounting, and monitoring are essential.

Faulty equipment, damaged batteries, inadequate protection, short circuits, incorrect wiring, overheating, improper charging, or water ingress can cause equipment damage, electric shock, burns, electric fire, serious injury, or death. Keep mains voltage out of this DIY controller. Mains-powered equipment and pressurized plumbing must use locally compliant components and qualified professional installation where required.

You are responsible for your build. Evaluate your components, design, local rules, and ability to do the work safely. This page documents a personal DIY project; it is not certified engineering advice and is provided without warranty. The project author accepts no liability for damage, loss, injury, or death resulting from its use.

This guide is visibly incompleteDo not infer wiring from prose or photographs. Several safety-critical schematics, ratings, pinouts, wire sizes, and enclosure measurements are still placeholders. Do not build the affected circuits until those documents have been completed and independently reviewed.

Step 1

Decide what you are building

Each enabled zone needs one latching valve and uses two soil-moisture sensors. One valid sensor can carry a zone if the other fails; if neither is valid, that zone is not watered. Every valve needs two momentary channels—one to open and one to close.

Before buying anything, record the number and position of plant groups, representative sensor locations, hose length and height, valve voltage and pulse requirements, and whether you want solar power, the display, server, and dashboard.

Step 2

Choose and prove the water source

Water must reach the valves with enough flow for the chosen endpoints. This installation uses elevated connected buckets, but a rainwater tank, regulated outlet, or controlled pump can work. Gravity systems have far less pressure than household plumbing.

  1. Connect one valve and the longest proposed hose without electronics.
  2. Open it manually and verify acceptable flow at every endpoint.
  3. Raise the reservoir or remove restrictions if flow is inadequate.
  4. Fit an accessible shutoff, keep water below the controller, and make drip loops.
  5. Verify every fitting’s pressure rating and test for leaks.

Step 5

Build the controller box

Plan for service, not compactness

Group fused power and distribution, keep the ESP32 reachable by USB, place each multiplexer near its connectors, separate valve wiring from analog sensor lines, provide drip loops, label both cable ends, and preserve access to removable parts.

Battery and solar hazardDisconnect and isolate every source before wiring. A battery can continue supplying destructive current even when the controller is off. Use correctly rated overcurrent protection close to the source, confirm polarity and conductor ratings, monitor initial charging and operation, and never use damaged or swollen equipment.

Power architecture documented so far

The battery positive lead passes through a 5 A fuse before the solar controller. Its protected 12 V load output feeds the valve contacts and a 12-to-5 V buck converter. The converter supplies sensors and relay coils; the ESP32 supplies 3.3 V to multiplexers and display. Four terminal chambers distribute common ground, 3.3 V, 5 V, and switched 12 V.

Do not wire from this descriptionThe fifth terminal chamber, exact fuse and wire ratings, terminal map, USB ground-switching relay, and complete fused power schematic are not documented. Wait for the reviewed schematic showing every connection and operating state.

Step 6

Bench-test before connecting water

Keep energy and water controlledDisconnect valve power or water first. Keep a manual shutoff within reach and never leave initial automatic cycles unattended.
  1. Verify supply voltages and polarity with the ESP32 removed.
  2. Power the ESP32 and inspect serial output at 115200 baud.
  3. Use the calibration sketch to map and calibrate every sensor.
  4. Use the relay test with water disconnected to prove every OPEN and CLOSE channel.
  5. Connect one valve at a time and verify direction and persistent state.
  6. With the enclosure protected and somebody present, conduct short leak and flow tests.
  7. Start automation with short durations and conservative thresholds.

Uploading or resetting the main firmware immediately starts an evaluation. An enabled dry zone may receive an OPEN command.

Step 7 · Mandatory software

Configure and install the firmware

The irrigation-firmware repository owns sensing, valve operation, cooldowns, safety decisions, and deep sleep.

git clone https://github.com/Jako9/irrigation-firmware.git
Set-Location irrigation-firmware
arduino-cli core update-index
arduino-cli core install esp32:esp32@3.3.11
arduino-cli lib install "ArduinoJson" "GxEPD2" "Adafruit GFX Library" "Adafruit BusIO"
Copy-Item .\secrets.h.example .\secrets.h

Set Wi-Fi, a random bearer secret, server URLs, battery-controller details, weather coordinates, timezone, and NTP server. Never commit secrets.h.

Calibrate every sensor

arduino-cli compile --fqbn "esp32:esp32:esp32" --library ".\calibrate" ".\calibrate"
arduino-cli upload --port COM4 --fqbn "esp32:esp32:esp32" ".\calibrate"
arduino-cli monitor --port COM4 --config baudrate=115200

Record stable wet and dry raw values through the finished wiring. Calibrate each actual sensor in representative soil; lower readings mean wetter soil.

Prove relay direction

arduino-cli compile --fqbn "esp32:esp32:esp32" ".\relay_test"
arduino-cli upload --port COM4 --fqbn "esp32:esp32:esp32" ".\relay_test"

The sketch cycles C0–C9. Disconnect water, map outputs, verify valve direction physically, and return every valve to a known closed state.

Configure zones and upload

Set both sensor channels and calibrations, OPEN/CLOSE channels, threshold, watering duration, and enabled state per installed zone. Leave unused zones disabled and verify evaluation interval, cooldown, pulse durations, valid raw range, and minimum battery percentage.

arduino-cli compile --fqbn "esp32:esp32:esp32:PartitionScheme=no_fs" `
  --library ".\LtbBluetooth" --library ".\display" "."
arduino-cli board list
arduino-cli upload --port COM4 `
  --fqbn "esp32:esp32:esp32:PartitionScheme=no_fs" "."

The first upload uses USB and the 4 MB no_fs partition scheme. Begin with every zone disabled, then prove one short, supervised OPEN/deadline/CLOSE sequence at a time.

Step 8 · Optional

Add the telemetry server

The irrigation-server stores telemetry and delivers configuration, commands, and OTA firmware. It requires Node.js 18.20+, SQLite, and persistent private storage.

git clone https://github.com/Jako9/irrigation-server.git
cd irrigation-server
npm ci
mkdir -p ./data/management
cp .env.example .env
cp config.example.json ./data/management/config.json

Use the same long bearer secret as the firmware, bind privately, allow only the controller subnet, and leave all zones disabled while validating. Run npm run check, post the supplied telemetry fixture, and inspect the database before connecting the ESP32. Adapt and verify the supplied systemd template for persistent deployment.

Step 9 · Optional

Add the browser dashboard

The irrigation-dashboard reads the server’s SQLite database and management directory. It adds history and protected administration but never replaces local watering logic.

git clone https://github.com/Jako9/irrigation-dashboard.git
cd irrigation-dashboard
npm ci
cp config.example.json config.json
npm run check

Configure timezone, expected interval, chart range, and zone aliases. Point both services at the same persistent data paths and start the irrigation server first. Keep the dashboard bound privately behind an authenticated proxy or tunnel. The supplied admin implementation expects Cloudflare Access and fails closed without all required settings.

Step 10

Bring the complete system online

Unattended operation is earned through testingAutomating water and stored electrical energy creates continuing risk. Monitor initial charging, temperature, enclosure moisture, wiring, valves, and watering cycles. Provide a manual water shutoff and a safe way to isolate the battery.
  1. With water off, verify sensor channels and valve directions.
  2. Physically close every valve.
  3. Start the optional server with all zones disabled, then the dashboard.
  4. Upload firmware by USB and inspect serial output.
  5. Verify battery, sensors, weather, server, and display independently.
  6. Enable one zone with a short duration while present.
  7. Confirm its physical closure and telemetry, then repeat zone by zone.
  8. Only after successful tests apply intended thresholds, durations, and cooldowns.

Deliberately test Wi-Fi loss, stopped services, one invalid sensor, and the manual water shutoff. Inspect reservoirs, hoses, connectors, valves, battery, solar equipment, and enclosure regularly.

Build deliberately

Start small and keep evidence

Photograph wiring, label everything, record calibration and test results, and add complexity only after the previous stage is proven.
